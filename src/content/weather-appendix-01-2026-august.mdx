## ...Synopsis...
Tropical Storm Dolly is not likely to cause significant impact to radio operations over the coming days. With limited time for Dolly to develop into a significant or notable system, and its transition to a post-tropical cyclone anticipated within the coming days, no significant wind threat is likley. The most likely impact, should one occur, is increased rainfall rates across the area if enough PWAT is able to develop across the area to contribute to increased rainfall rates. 

A front will move through in the short term and contribute to increased thunderstorm and rainshower activity. This activity will likely limit the ability for radio operations to occur, unless they occur in the morning to early afternoon hours. Isolated strong to severe storms may be possible with any of these storms as sea breeze collisions occur with storms forming along the front, but the likelihood of severe weather is incredibly low. 

A particularly notable heatwave is expected into the early September period and will cause an extreme heat risk to develop. Florida, however, should not face the brunt of this, but increased temperatures may still be possible across the area. Those who are susceptible to extreme heat should avoid being outdoors during peak heating hours if possible, and those engaging in prolonged outdoor activity should seek to hydrate or frequently locate shade or move indoors to cool down. 

## ...Discussion...
Tropical Storm Dolly is expected to face conditions unfavorable for development as it continues westward toward the Leeward Islands. As Tropical Storm Dolly transitions into a post-tropical cyclone, its remnants may potentially be able to move toward the Florida peninsula and subsequently become responsible for increased PWAT values across the area, which could lead to increased rainfall and the potential for flash flooding should this scenario occur. However, confidence in this scenario is incredibly low, and it is most likely Tropical Storm Dolly and its remnants will not affect the Florida peninsula. 

Ensemble models currently depict a trend of increased ridging by early September, which may contribute to increased temperatures across the area. Both the EPS and GEFS depict a heatwave, and this is in accordance with the CPC's latest guidance regarding the potential heatwave across the CONUS in early September. Extreme temperatures should remain away from the Florida peninsula and should mostly be confined to the midwest and great plains. However, increased temperatures could still become hazardous to those operating outdoors should one be susceptible to extreme heat or not be sufficiently hydrated. 

The current level of heat is expected to remain possibly out to September 14th, although confidence is low on this possibility, but at least out to the 10th, extreme heat may be possible. Outside of that, model trends do not depict invigorated rainfall across the peninsula, and lower precipitation chances may follow in the subsequent days preceding the release of this post when analyzing 6 hour QPF. However, higher moisture levels, signified with PWAT being >2" within the atmosphere, may contribute to higher rainfall amounts, and especially rates, and should contribute to increased thunderstorm potential for at least the next little while. The most favorable time of day to conduct radio operations would therefore be in the early morning and early afternoon, before storms have the chance to form and cause issues to operations. 

In the short-term, a front will progress across the peninsula and will cause invigorated thunderstorm and rain shower activity across the peninsula with the potential for some limited and isolated organization of thunderstorms to strong to severe strength. As was seen in recent events, thunderstorms capable of 50 MPH gusts (or higher) have become more probable due to this alignment of ingredients favorable for their development. With incredibly high CAPE, high PWAT, and a trigger, storms will remain likely for the next little while. As was stated, only isolated thunderstorms may be capable of developing to strong to severe strength due to the lack of sufficient wind shear across the area, which would otherwise favor better storm development. But, since this ingredient is lacking, severe weather is not a given with the development of any storms.
 
On all other fronts, conditions will likely continue to be nominal and supportive of radio operations. Once more, the most favorable time currently appears to be in the morning, before the formation of thunderstorms and rain.
