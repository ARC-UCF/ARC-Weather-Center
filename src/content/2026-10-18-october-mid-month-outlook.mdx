## ...Synopsis...

**Conditions are favorable for outdoor radio operation, with the requirement for some mitigation action.**

A cold front has passed over the Florida peninsula, and with it, has brought down the temperature and moved away humidity. This generally favors cooler temperatures, and accordingly, cooler temperatures are expected to remain through the rest of this month, with a not high likelihood of temperatures exceeding 89 degrees. Temperatures will gradually warm, but will remain in the 70s for the next week or so. 

The cold front has also brought with it wind assosciated with a relatively strong pressure gradient that has developed by a high pressure building behind the low which produced this cold front. This gradient is producing these higher wind gusts, and is expected to continue to producing relatively higher wind gusts over the course of the week. Even so, these wind gusts will remain neglible and can be easily mitigated by ensuring antennas are properly fastened and secured, with no risk of being blown over by the wind. 

However, by the latter end of this month, the potential for a higher wind gust event could materialize. The extent to which this threat will develop is unknown, but current model guidance indicates an extreme around 50 MPH. There is still time for this estimate to shift. The GFS and GEFS do not favor this high-end of a scenario, however, and due to model variability, the confidence in such a event is low.

## ...Discussion...
A cold front has passed over the Florida peninsula, and has brought with it seasonably cooler temperatures and lower humidity/dewpoints, thus reducing the oppressive feel in the atmosphere. Further, temperatures are expected to decrease over time and the lows will continue to drop over the coming days. However, the highs will continue to climb, but will not exceed 89 degrees. Temperatures are likely to remain in the mid to upper 70s through the rest of this week, and the potential for generally higher temperatures, but still cool, will not occur until closer to the end of this month, **if** it is to occur at all. 

The cold front will continue to produce generally higher wind gusts, in the range of 25 to 35 MPH, through the rest of this week. Gusty winds could become hazardous to antennas should they not be properly fastened and supported against the wind. These windspeeds are otherwise not dangerous, and are typical for this time of year. 

Among the benefits of the passing cold front, little to no precipitation is anticipated in the coming days, until Saturday, where chances for precipitation and thunderstorms will return, but are anticipated to be of low chances and not expected to be widespread in coverage. These may be general systems of light to moderate precipitation that convect in the morning to afternoon or evening hours and then spread across central Florida, producing small amounts of rain. Otherwise, precipitation is not anticipated to be hazardous or pose any threat to outdoor operations. 

However, in the 8 to 14 day period, there exists the possibility for high wind. The extent of this threat is yet to be finalized, and is dependent on how conditions develop over the coming days and the solutions models produce. This risk has been indicated by the Climate Prediction Center over the period from the 25th to the 29th of October, by the latter end of this month. Based upon current model analysis, the ECWMF is producing a higher-end scenario, with wind speeds near 50 MPH in some locations, with sustained winds ~25 MPH. The ECWMF ENS has been in support of the deterministic solution. However, the GFS and GEFS do not support the higher-end solution, and support relatively more moderate gusts, around 30 to 35, close to what is currently being experienced. 

An update on the situation regarding wind gusts will be provided closer to the beginning of the period, or closer to the start of any hazardous conditions, if the conditions warrant such an update. Otherwise, the next monthly summary will not be issued until October 31st, which will summarize conditions as we proceed into November.
