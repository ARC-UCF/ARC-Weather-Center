## ...Synopsis...

There exists some potential for severe weather to occur beginning of November, through possibly to Friday the first week of November. However, confidence in this scenario is low at the moment, due to model variability and how far out into the future the event remains. 

Poor to modest conditions for severe weather development will primarily exist, likely providing for no more than a marginal severe weather threat across the peninsula. Current GFS soundings indicate less than favorable low-level SRH for tornado development, and modest SRH in the upper levels of the atmosphere. Modest CAPE (around 1500 J/kg) and modest lapse rates around 6-6.5 degrees Celsius will likely limit the formation ability of any storms. Further, poor wind shear around 25-30kts will limit development ability for severe weather. 

## ...Discussion...

There exists potential for some severe weather to occur beginning of November, through possibly to Friday the first week of November. As of current, the GFS supports this scenario within the 189 hour range (marginally outside the 172 hour range for 7 days). This scenario is, albeit, not guaranteed to occur due to how far out in time it's currently being forecasted, however, there does seem to be some plausibility in a marignal severe weather risk as a result. 

Low-level SRH (less than 50m^2/s^2) is unfavorable for major tornado development; modest SRH (approx. 150m^2/s^2) appears to exist within a modest CAPE environment (around 1500 J/kg) with modest lapse rates around 6-6.5 degrees Celsius in the upper levels of the atmopshere around the beginning of the severe weather period. Weaker wind shear aloft will likely limit the ability for severe thunderstorms to form and organize, but brief pockets of around 35-40 kts as depicted by the GFS could support some brief multi-cellular organization; supercell organization is unlikely, and only marginal supercell structures will occur, if they occur at all. Wind shear aloft will likely be between 25-30kts, fueled by a trough which will dive south across the southeast. A secondary trough may succeed the first one, which may dive further south, and ind shear will overspread the peninsula a few days following the first trough's arrival, but confidence in this scenario is incredibly low due to model variability and how far out into the future it is. 

If such a scenario were to occur, it may last from November 2nd to November 5th or 6th, depending on arrival times and the development of other features across the CONUS and near or around Florida in the coming week or so.
