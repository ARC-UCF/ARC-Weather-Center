Two tropical AOIs (areas of interest) have appeared with locations close in proximity to the Florida peninsula. As a result, these tropical AOIs will need to be monitored for any potential threat to the Florida peninsula, and consequently, any potential threat to outdoor and radio operations. These hazards could potentially include high wind gusts, flooding rainfall leading to flash flooding, the chance for increased severe weather activity in accordance with any tropical cyclone development, and potentially heightened rainshower and thunderstorm activity. 

The biggest of these threats could be any flash flooding potential: regardless of the strength of the tropical cyclone, a tropical cyclone of any size could increase PWAT values across the Florida peninsula, which would then contribute to increased rainfall rates which could then contribute to flooding rainfall. Any storms which are able to train across the area or remain stationary for prolonged periods of time (upwards of 2 hours or more) with any organized convection would then pose such a risk to outdoor operations to the extent the rainfall could damage equipment or potentially become life-threatening under the right circumstances. 

Outside of this, even a tropical depression or tropical cyclone attempting to develop has the potential to increase severe weather potential across the area. The spin of these systems is often time able to trigger marginal organized convection, but in rare circumstances, can cause significantly more organized convection which would warrant severe weather watches, but whether or not this will occur is yet to be determined and relies on the exact track of any developing systems and their intensity. 

Outside of tropical hazards, an extreme heat event is becoming increasingly likely to develop. Per the latest GFS, heat index values up to 110, or even higher, potentially up to 117, could occur across the area, with minimal heat relief in the later hours of the day. This would occur in the early days of September. Therefore, individuals looking to conduct outdoor operations during this period should be aware of this potential. A much more definitive threat can be determined close to the actual appearance of the threat, but current trends are beginning to line up with the potential for extreme heat. Should this threat develop, it is considered that any outdoor operations during this period, regardless of how brief or long it is, should not be conducted. Outdoor operations should be moved indoors, or done outside of peak heating hours, which are between 11am and 5pm. Should one need to work during this heat wave, you should do so as is strictly necessary, and remember to remain hydrated. Heat index values this high can cause heat illness even in those who are not susceptible to heat. **This threat is currently conditional, and may or may not develop.
